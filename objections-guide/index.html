01 / Start here: ask first

Before you answer any objection, ask a question.

Most objections are a cover for something underneath. "I don't have the money" might mean they're scared, their partner said no, or they don't see the value yet. If you answer the surface objection, you miss the real one. So ask first, listen, then use the answer below that fits.

The question that works for every objection

What makes you say that?

When they've already decided no

Before you make up your mind, would it make sense to see all the information so you know what you're saying no to?

When they need "more time to think"

Just out of curiosity, what part are you still thinking through?
Two rules for every answer

Never promise income, and never promise health results. Talk about your own experience, what you're building, and why. If a lead wants numbers or research, the discovery process and the 1:1 call are where they get the full picture.

02 / "What is this?"

"What is the business?" / "What do you do?"

I help people start an online business, even with no experience (I had none when I started). You get the training, high-ticket products to sell, and a sales system that runs 24/7. Your part is learning the process and bringing interested people in, and the system does a lot of the work from there. The products are in-home water machines from Enagic, a company that's been around for over 50 years. Because they're high-ticket, each sale pays a real commission, so you don't need hundreds of customers. That's the basic overview. Does that make sense?

"What are the products?"

We work with Enagic's water machines. They make water for drinking, cooking, and cleaning, all from one machine in your kitchen, and they help cut down on plastic bottles too. The discovery process has a full breakdown: the products, a demo, the compensation plan, and how we automate the business. Then you get a 1:1 call where you can ask anything. It's so hard to do it justice over a message, so that's where you get the whole picture. Does that make sense?

03 / Money

"I don't have the money to invest."

Use the back-and-forth in the TEMPLATE | Lead To Sign Up Verbiage ("They watched the webclass and say they don't have money to invest"). Start with "What makes you say that?" first.

"It's too expensive."

Ask first: "What makes you feel it's too expensive?" Then go deeper. Find the objection, find what's under it, and help them talk about how that's affecting their life.

I hear you, I felt the same way at first. Something that helped me: almost every product you buy has marketing costs built into the price. Enagic doesn't have a big marketing budget. Instead, it pays commissions to the people who share the machines, and it only pays when a sale happens. The machines also replace a lot of things in a home: bottled water, cleaning products, and more, for years. When you look at it that way, the price looks different. There are lots of financing options too, so nobody has to pay it all upfront. We go through all of that on the 1:1 call.

"How fast will I earn back my investment?" / "How quickly can I start making money?"

Use "How fast will I see results?" in the TEMPLATE | Lead To Sign Up Verbiage. It's honest about timelines and ends with a question back to them.

"How much money do you make?"

I get why you'd ask! This is a real business, and like any business, results come from learning and taking action. [Share where you are honestly: what you've done so far, or what you're building and why.] Everyone's results are different, so the best way to see what's possible for you is the discovery process and a 1:1 call. What kind of income would make a difference for you right now?

"I have to wait until payday."

Totally fine! When do you get paid, so I can check back in with you then?

"I invested in a business before and lost money, so I'm scared to try again."

I completely understand, and I'm glad you told me. Can I ask what happened with that business? A few things that made me feel safer here: Enagic is a direct sales company, not an MLM. You don't have to build a team to rank up, you can rank by your own sales, and you never rank down, so if life gets busy you keep the rank you've earned. There are no monthly orders or quotas. The discovery process is there so you can see everything before you put any real money in. Would that help you feel more sure either way?

04 / Time and skills

"I don't have the time."

I hear you. This is something you'd have to make a priority if you're serious about it. We have people on the team with young kids, full-time jobs, or both, and they find the time because it matters to them. The upside is the system does a lot of the work. Once your ads and automation are set up, leads come in and go through the information on their own time, so you're not holding everyone's hand. You still have to put energy in, but it's built to work around a busy life. How much time could you realistically give it each week?

"I don't want to quit my job too early."

That's how I felt when I got started too. You don't have to quit your job. Lots of people build this on the side while working full time, and there's no set timeline. You decide when, and if, it makes sense for you.

"I have no skills or experience."

That's okay, most of us didn't either. The platform teaches you marketing and sales step by step, and you have a team and community to ask when you're stuck. You don't need to know how to do it before you start. You learn it as you go.

"I don't know how to build a brand or a business."

You don't have to figure it out alone. When I started, I didn't know how to do anything online and I felt overwhelmed. What changed it for me was having a platform that showed me each step. Inside you get your onboarding steps, mentorship and community support, mindset training, branding and social media, ads and automation, sales training, and how to set up your own funnel. Plus weekly team calls. Would it help to see it all in the discovery process?

"I'll think about it."

Of course! When's a good time for me to check back in with you?

05 / Is this legit, a scam, or an MLM?

"Is this legit?" / "This seems too good to be true."

That's a fair question, and I'm glad you're careful. Here's what made me comfortable: Enagic has been around for over 50 years and sells a real product. You're not starting from scratch, because the product, shipping, customer service, and training already exist. You get paid when you sell a product, not for signing people up. It still takes real work, and nobody can promise you results. But it's a real business. What would help you feel sure about it?

"This is a scam."

Ask first: "What's making you say that? Have you been scammed before?" If they've made up their mind, you're not there to convince them. These points are there if they're open.

I know it can be scary to look into an online business, because there are so many stories out there. You can find someone calling almost any business a scam. This one has been around for over 50 years, and the people who do well in it are the ones who show up and do the work. There's never a guarantee when you start a business. If you've decided it's not for you, I respect that completely. And if you want to keep exploring, I'm happy to keep sharing.

"Is this an MLM?"

Ask first: "Have you been in an MLM before? What was that like?" Then go from there.

It's not an MLM. Enagic is a direct sales company with its own compensation plan. With most MLMs you need to build a team to rank up, and you have to hit quotas or monthly orders to keep your rank. Here, everyone starts at the same place. Your rank comes from sales, and you can reach the top rank on your own. A team can help you get there faster, but it's not required. Once you hit a rank, you keep it, there's no ranking down. And there are no monthly orders. Does that make sense?

"Is this a pyramid scheme?"

No. Pyramid schemes are illegal, and they're built on recruiting people to put money in without a real product. Enagic sells a physical product, and we earn commissions when a product is sold. A company that's been around for over 50 years in many countries couldn't run that way. Your income here comes from sales, and you can rank all the way up without building a team.

06 / Selling and what people think

"I don't want to sell." / "I can't sell."

I get that! Something that helped me: we all sell, every day. When you tell a friend about a great movie or where you got your shirt, you're selling, you just don't get paid for it. When you apply for a job, you're selling yourself. The platform teaches you how to share in a way that doesn't feel pushy. And learning sales is a skill that helps you in anything you do, whether or not you join.

"I don't want to sell to friends and family."

That's totally fine! We use ads and social media to reach people who are already looking for what we offer. You don't have to message your friends and family to make this work.

"I don't want to be salesy."

Totally understand, neither do I. We run this like a real business. Our ads go to people who are already interested in starting an online business, so we're not cold messaging people about a random product. We never pressure anyone, and the platform teaches you how to do the same.

"I'm afraid of what other people will think."

That's so normal, I felt it too. This is your own journey. Other people don't have to understand it for it to be right for you. What would it mean for you if this worked?

"How do you find clients and business partners?"

It depends on what you're comfortable with. The platform teaches both. Organic is free: you use social media and the people you already know. Paid is ads: they're shown to people who fit your ideal client on social media, so you're not reaching out to anyone who isn't interested. If talking to friends and family makes you uncomfortable, you don't have to.

"Are there enough people who want to buy these?" / "Will the market dry up?"

That's a good question. Everyone drinks water, and most people have never heard of these machines. With billions of people on social media, and ads that reach the people who are actually interested, there's a lot of room.

"I don't think it's for me."

No worries! I understand being skeptical, most people are about online work these days. I only want to share this with people who see the value in it. If you're ever curious in the future, I'm here. Do you mind sharing what made you feel it's not for you? It helps me for the future.

"No, I'm not interested."

Thank you so much for letting me know, I really appreciate it! If you ever change your mind, I'm here. Until then, I hope you find something that feels right for you. If you don't mind me asking, what made you decide not to move forward? Just so I know for the future.

07 / Partner and family

"I have to talk to my partner." / "My partner doesn't believe in it."

A real person with a real story usually helps more than facts. If your lead and their partner are open to it, connect them with someone on the team whose story fits theirs (your mentor, or someone who started in a similar spot).

That makes total sense, it's a big decision and you should make it together. If you're excited about this and your partner is open to learning more, I'd love to get you both on a chat with [name]. [Their story: why they started, and what it's been like.] Hearing it from a real person usually answers a lot of questions. Would that help?

08 / The water

Keep these about your own experience and what the machine does. Don't make health claims. If a lead wants research, let them know it's covered in the discovery process.

"It's impossible for one machine to do all that."

I thought the same thing at first! I use mine for [drinking, cooking, cleaning, washing produce, laundry]. What makes you think it can't? I can take a quick video of my machine and show you the settings, or send you a demo.

"All water is the same." / "I don't believe in this water."

I get that, I was skeptical too. What made you feel that way? I can only share my own experience: [what you've noticed using it in your home]. If you're curious, I'm happy to send you a demo so you can see it for yourself and decide.

"I looked it up and the machine costs thousands. I don't have that kind of money."

Yes, it's a big investment, and I felt the same way when I started! Most of us didn't have that money sitting around. There are a lot of financing options so you can pay monthly, and we walk through them on the 1:1 call to find what fits you.
